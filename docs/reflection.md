# Reflection

The hardest design decision was making the programme realistic for warehouse staff, remote sales teams and a small security function at the same time. A long annual course could be easy to administer but would not show whether people can recognise invoice fraud, use the reporting route or apply secure behaviour in daily work. Repeated simulations can provide useful behavioural evidence, but they can also create distrust if they are used to name and shame staff.

I therefore selected short role-based activities, supported by practical examples and supportive follow-up. This creates a trade-off: it requires managers to protect time for shift briefings and the security team to review several evidence sources. The programme addresses this by limiting major simulation campaigns to three, using existing supervisors and HR records, and holding quarterly reviews that focus on decisions rather than presentation of metrics. The main uncertainty is the baseline. Low reporting could mean low awareness, poor trust or few attacks, so no single metric is treated as proof of programme success.
